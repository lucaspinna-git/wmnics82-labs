Passwords and Encryption

Authentication • Password Management • Cryptography

Physical locks safeguard physical property such as vehicles and residences. In the digital world, where personal identities, bank accounts, and corporate confidential data exist as bits across networks, authentication and passwords provide the foundational boundary of cybersecurity.

Authentication is the process of verifying that an entity—a human user, a service account, or a networked device—is genuinely who or what it claims to be.

The Five Recognized Factors of Authentication
1. Something You Know
Passwords, passphrases, personal identification numbers (PINs), security answers.
2. Something You Have
Physical smartcards, USB hardware tokens (YubiKey), time-based authenticator apps (TOTP).
3. Something You Are
Biometric traits: fingerprint geometry, facial recognition scans, retinal patterns.
4. Somewhere You Are (Location)
IP address geofencing, GPS coordinates restricting access to authorized physical offices.
5. Something You Do (Behavioural)
Keystroke dynamics, touchscreen swipe velocity, and signature stylus pressure patterns.
CompTIA Password Complexity Standards (MD 2.3.3)

A strong password is mathematically difficult for automated attack scripts (brute force and dictionary attacks) to crack. Robust credentials mandate four character classes:

Uppercase: A – Z
Lowercase: a – z
Numbers: 0 – 9
Symbols: ! @ # $ % ^ & *

Length vs Complexity: Increasing length exponentially increases cryptographic entropy. A 16-character passphrase composed of random words (e.g. Cobalt#Meadow!Velvet9) is significantly more resilient against GPU-accelerated hashing clusters than an 8-character string with complex symbols.

Modern digital citizens interact with hundreds of password-protected web platforms, enterprise applications, and cloud accounts. Reusing credentials across services creates extreme vulnerability: if one gaming forum suffers a data breach, cybercriminals automatically execute credential stuffing attacks against banking and email portals using the leaked username and password.

Dedicated Password Managers

Software tools (e.g. Bitwarden, 1Password) that store credentials inside a zero-knowledge AES-256 encrypted vault. Generates unique 20+ character passwords for every website and auto-fills them securely.

Browser Password Vaults

Integrated password managers inside Chrome, Safari, or Edge. Convenient, but risks total account compromise if an unauthorized user gains physical access to an unlocked computer.

Insecure Plaintext Storage

Writing passwords on sticky notes attached to monitor bezels, or saving them in unencrypted text files named passwords.txt on the desktop. Completely exposes credentials to physical and malware theft.

CompTIA Evaluation: When Should You Change a Password? (MD 2.3.5)

Historically, IT departments forced mandatory 90-day password resets. Modern NIST (National Institute of Standards and Technology) standards have debunked this practice because frequent forced changes cause users to adopt predictable patterns (e.g. Winter2026! → Spring2026!).

Mandatory Change Triggers
  • When an organization or service notifies you of a data breach.
  • If you notice unauthorized account activity or unusual logins.
  • If you suspect your credentials have been seen or compromised.
Proactive Hardening Triggers
  • Replacing old, short, or letter-only legacy passwords.
  • Eliminating repeated passwords shared across multiple accounts.
  • Upgrading an account containing highly sensitive financial or medical PII.

Encryption transforms clearly understood human writing, known as plaintext, into an indecipherable scramble called ciphertext. Encryption ensures that even if an unauthorized party intercepts a message or steals a physical hard drive, they cannot read the underlying information without the specific cryptographic key.

Historical Cryptography: The Julius Caesar Shift Cipher (MD 2.3.1)

One of the earliest recorded military uses of encryption occurred in ancient Rome. Roman Emperor Julius Caesar scrambled written battle orders sent to his frontline commanders to prevent Germanic and Gallic scouts from learning his tactical movements:

Plaintext: ATTACK AT DAWN
Algorithm: Shift every letter forward in alphabet by 3 positions (Key = +3)
Ciphertext: DWWDFN DW GDZQ

Caesar's generals possessed the secret key (-3 offset) and could easily shift the characters backward to read the orders. To an interceptor lacking the key, the scroll appeared as total nonsense.

Data in Transit (Motion)

Information travelling across public cables, wireless links, and routers. Protected using Transport Layer Security (HTTPS), SFTP, and VPN tunnels so packet-sniffers capture only ciphertext.

Data at Rest (Storage)

Inactive data residing on hard disk drives, SSDs, database records, and backup tapes. Protected using Full Disk Encryption (BitLocker, FileVault) so a stolen laptop cannot be accessed by removing its drive.

Modern computational cryptography is split into two primary architectures: Symmetric (shared secret) and Asymmetric (public key infrastructure).

Symmetric Encryption
Single Secret Key

Both the sender and the receiver share the exact same cryptographic key to both encrypt and decrypt the payload.

  • Primary Standard: AES (Advanced Encryption Standard - 128/256 bit).
  • Strengths: Extremely fast and computationally lightweight; ideal for bulk file encryption and streaming data.
  • Core Limitation: The Key Exchange Problem: How do you safely transmit the secret key to the receiver over an untrusted network?
Asymmetric Encryption
Public/Private Key Pair

Utilises mathematically linked key pairs: a Public Key (published openly) and a Private Key (kept secret by the owner).

  • Primary Standards: RSA, Elliptic Curve Cryptography (ECC).
  • Mechanism: Anyone can encrypt a message using your Public Key, but ONLY your Private Key can decrypt it.
  • Strengths: Solves key distribution over public channels; powers SSL/TLS handshakes and digital signatures.

With cyberattacks and regulatory enforcement expanding worldwide, specialization in data privacy and cryptography represents one of the highest-demand career trajectories in modern IT.

Data Privacy Attorney / Legal Counsel

Legal specialists who advise corporations on compliance with statutory frameworks (UK GDPR, CCPA, HIPAA). They draft data processing agreements and defend against regulatory enforcement actions.

Data Privacy Specialist / DPO

Technical compliance leads overseeing institutional data governance. They conduct Privacy Impact Assessments (PIAs), audit database access permissions, and manage Data Subject Access Requests (DSARs).

Data Mining Analyst

Technical analysts examining vast enterprise datasets to discover patterns and business intelligence, ensuring data anonymisation and pseudo-anonymisation standards are maintained.

Identify Internet Basics

Decentralised Network • WWW Architecture • Safe Browsing

Prior to the digital age, humans accessed recorded knowledge through physical repositories: municipal libraries, paper encyclopaedias, microfiche projection reels, and postal correspondence. Sharing information across international borders took weeks.

The Internet revolutionized human connectivity by establishing a decentralised global network of interconnected computer networks. No single corporation, president, or nation-state controls the Internet. Instead, thousands of public and private networks communicate seamlessly by conforming to open, vendor-neutral communications protocols known as the TCP/IP suite.

CompTIA Architecture Analogy: The Global Highway System (MD 3.1.2)

Think of the Internet as a vast international superhighway system spanning oceans and continents:

1. The Roads & Bridges
Physical undersea fiber optic cables, satellite links, cell towers, and core enterprise routing switches.
2. The Traffic & Cargo
Discrete packets carrying emails, streaming video chunks, video game telemetry, and financial wires.
3. The Highway Code
TCP/IP protocols governing addressing (IP) and guaranteed packet delivery (TCP) without collisions.

A frequent point of confusion for new technologists is treating the "Internet" and the "World Wide Web" as interchangeable synonyms. They are fundamentally distinct layers of computing architecture:

The Internet (Underlying Infrastructure)

The massive physical and logical networking infrastructure connecting billions of devices worldwide. Includes routers, switches, fiber cables, IP addressing schemes, and domain routing.

Analogy: The Railway Tracks & Signals
The World Wide Web • WWW (Service Layer)

An application-layer information service invented by Sir Tim Berners-Lee in 1989. A collection of multimedia documents interconnected by clickable hyperlinks, accessed via HTTP/HTTPS.

Analogy: One Passenger Train on the Tracks
Other Trains Running on the Internet Tracks:
• Email (SMTP / IMAP / POP3) • Voice over IP (VoIP telephony) • File Transfers (SFTP / FTPS) • Video Streaming (RTSP) • Online Multiplayer Gaming

To navigate web resources, every document possesses a standardized address: the Uniform Resource Locator (URL).

Anatomy of a Uniform Resource Locator (URL)
https:// www. netcomtraining.co.uk /courses/tech-plus
Scheme: HTTPS (Encrypted)
Subdomain: World Wide Web
Domain: Server Hostname
Path: File / Resource Route
Domain Name System (DNS): The Phonebook of the Internet

Computers route data across the Internet using numerical IP addresses (e.g. 192.0.2.45), but human brains remember names (e.g. netcomtraining.co.uk). DNS servers resolve friendly domain names into machine-routable IP addresses within milliseconds.

When navigating the public web, client devices transmit sensitive login tokens, banking credentials, and private messages. Ensuring transmission security prevents malicious eavesdropping and credential harvesting.

HTTPS & The Browser Padlock Icon

Hypertext Transfer Protocol Secure encrypts all HTTP traffic using TLS. Clicking the browser padlock reveals the digital certificate issued by a trusted Certificate Authority (CA), verifying server identity.

Typosquatting & Deceptive Domains

Cybercriminals register misspelled domains (e.g. paypa1.com instead of paypal.com) mimicking legitimate corporate portals to deceive users into disclosing passwords.

Using a Web Browser

Profiles • Extensions • Cache Maintenance

A web browser is the primary portal through which users interact with cloud applications. Modern browsers allow extensive customization to optimize ergonomics, focus, and security.

CompTIA Architecture: The Browser Profile Sandbox (MD 3.2.1)

A Browser Profile is an isolated environment containing its own unique set of bookmarks, browser extensions, stored passwords, browsing history, and cookies:

Work Profile
Signed into corporate SSO, Microsoft 365, internal VPN bookmarks, enterprise security extensions.
Study Profile (Netcom)
Configured for Canvas LMS, CompTIA CertMaster, coding repos, and course-specific bookmarks.
Personal Profile
Personal social media, streaming entertainment, and domestic utility account logins.

Browser Extensions (Add-ons) are modular software packages that extend the native capabilities of a browser (e.g. ad-blockers, translation tools, grammar checkers, password managers).

Security Architecture: The Threat of Extension Privilege Bloat (MD 3.2.3)

Many extensions request permission to "Read and change all your data on the websites you visit". This gives the extension code access to inspect form inputs, capture credentials, read financial records, and monitor web browsing.

Supply Chain Attacks
Benign extensions purchased by malicious entities can be updated with stealth keyloggers without user awareness.
Enterprise Best Practice
Install only verified extensions from official stores; immediately remove unneeded or abandoned add-ons.

A critical CompTIA exam objective is debunking common misconceptions regarding Private Browsing (Incognito Mode).

What Incognito Actually Does (Local Privacy)
  • Does not write visited web URLs to your local browsing history.
  • Deletes temporary cookies when the private window is closed.
  • Discards temporary cache files and form auto-fill data upon exit.
  • Prevents other users of the same physical computer from seeing your session.
What Incognito Does NOT Do (Network Reality)
  • Does NOT make you anonymous to your Internet Service Provider (ISP).
  • Does NOT hide your traffic from employer network monitoring or firewalls.
  • Does NOT prevent visited web servers from tracking your public IP address.
  • Does NOT shield your computer from downloading malware or malicious files.

Maintaining browser health is vital for system speed, stability, and safeguarding against zero-day exploit vulnerabilities.

Browser Maintenance: Cache vs. Cookies vs. History (MD 3.2.5)
Browser Cache
Locally saved static web assets (images, CSS styles, JS files). Speeds up page loads. If a website renders broken or fails to update, clearing cache forces a fresh download.
HTTP Cookies
Small key-value text tokens storing login authentication sessions and cart states. Clearing cookies logs you out of all active web sessions across all tabs.
Browsing History
Chronological database of visited URLs. Clearing history removes search suggestions and visit timestamps from the local device.

An Internet Service Provider (ISP) is a telecommunications company that maintains direct physical connections to Tier 1 Internet backbones, routing subscriber data across municipal and international networks.

The Customer Premises Equipment (CPE) Ecosystem
Modem
Modulates/demodulates analog RF signals or telephone tones into digital binary Ethernet frames.
ONT (Fibre Terminal)
Optical Network Terminal converting incoming light pulses from optical fiber into electrical Ethernet signals.
Router
Routes IP packets between local home devices (LAN) and the ISP network (WAN) via NAT and DHCP.
Integrated Gateway
All-in-one box combining modem/ONT, router, multi-port Ethernet switch, and Wi-Fi access point.

Cable broadband utilizes the existing coaxial copper cable infrastructure originally deployed to distribute analog television signals into neighbourhoods.

Anatomy of a Coaxial Cable (Slide 30 & MD 3.3.3)
1. Central Conductor
Solid copper wire carrying high-frequency RF data signals.
2. Dielectric Insulator
Foam polyethylene maintaining exact distance and electrical spacing.
3. Braided Shielding
Metallic foil & copper mesh blocking electromagnetic interference (EMI).
4. Outer Jacket
Durable plastic sheath protecting against weather and abrasion.
The Shared Neighbourhood Loop Reality:
Cable broadband connects hundreds of homes to a shared local optical node. Because bandwidth is pooled across the street, subscriber throughput frequently drops during peak evening hours (7:00pm – 10:00pm) when neighbours stream 4K video simultaneously.

Digital Subscriber Line (DSL) transmits high-speed data over traditional copper twisted-pair telephone lines (PSTN) that were installed decades ago for analog voice calls.

Frequency Separation

Voice telephone calls use acoustic frequencies between 300Hz and 3.4kHz. DSL transmits high-frequency data signals (25kHz up to several MHz) on the same copper pair without causing interference, allowing voice calls and Internet to operate simultaneously.

Distance Attenuation Limits

High frequencies deteriorate rapidly over copper wire. DSL speeds drop sharply the further your home is located from the telephone exchange or street cabinet (DSLAM). Customers beyond 3 miles experience significant degradation.

Fibre optic broadband represents the pinnacle of transmission technology, replacing traditional copper electrical wiring with flexible strands of pure glass or plastic as thin as a human hair.

Physical Law: Total Internal Reflection (MD 3.3.9)

Data is converted by lasers into pulses of light. As light travels down the inner glass core, it strikes the outer glass cladding at an angle greater than the critical angle, bouncing endlessly forward without escaping:

Blistering Speeds
From 100 Mbps up to 25 Gbps, with identical symmetric upload and download bandwidth.
Immune to EMI
Because light pulses carry no electrical charge, fibre is completely immune to radio interference, lightning, and electrical noise.
Ultra-Low Latency
Ping times of 1ms – 10ms, delivering instant responsiveness for video conferencing and real-time operations.

Where laying physical street cabling is economically impractical or geographically impossible, wireless broadband provides essential connectivity.

Fixed Wireless Access • 4G/5G FWA

Uses the same cellular towers that communicate with mobile phones. A home router fitted with a high-gain cellular antenna and SIM card receives broadband (40 – 300+ Mbps) without needing a street trench.

Satellite: GEO vs LEO (Starlink)
Geostationary (GEO - 35,786 km): High latency (~600ms) due to speed of light round-trip. Causes major lag during phone and video calls.
Low Earth Orbit (LEO - ~550 km): Megaconstellations (e.g. Starlink) providing 50 – 250 Mbps with low latency (25 – 45ms), transforming rural and maritime access.

Internet infrastructure requires multidisciplinary engineering talent to build, splice, configure, and monitor global communications pipelines.

Broadband Field Engineer

Deploys customer premises equipment, splices fiber optic pigtails, tests optical loss with OTDR meters, and certifies customer termination.

NOC Analyst (Network Operations)

24/7 telemetry monitoring of core ISP routing nodes, detecting fiber cuts, managing BGP route peering, and coordinating emergency response.

Web Infrastructure Developer

Software engineers programming DNS routing, CDN caching layers, and high-availability cloud application gateways.
